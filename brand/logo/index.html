Crucible mark

The mouth of a Hessian assay crucible: a three-cornered rim seen from above, with the melt inside. A master drawing, separate 32 and 16 pixel cuts, a one-colour set, and a lockup with Manrope SemiBold. Every picture on this sheet is drawn from the files in this folder.

Only the mouthThe body and foot are gone. What is left is the rim, a sliver of its outer wall, the back wall inside and the orange melt.
Seen from 62°High enough that the mouth is the whole mark, low enough that the back is foreshortened and the melt sits below the rim.
Turned 15°No edge is level and no corner points straight down, so it no longer reads as a downvote arrow or a yield sign.
Sharp cornersRim, back wall and melt are sharp triangles with parallel sides; the lip is the rim's edge dropped straight down.

Construction

The mouth is modelled in 3D and projected: a triangular rim slab, its outer wall, and the melt 0.45 of the rim's radius down inside it. The eye is 62° above the horizon and the triangle is turned 15° off its near corner, so no edge is level and the near corner does not point straight down. Corners are sharp: each outline is one core triangle grown with mitred corners, so sides stay parallel.

Solid lines: the rim's outer and inner triangles. Dashed: the melt before it is clipped by the rim, and the foot of the lip. Dots: the rim's corners.

Grid
64 units. The mark fills 56 by 56, from 4 to 60 both ways.
Corners
(4.2, 16.5), (59.8, 4) and the near corner (46.8, 57). Every edge is at least 12° from level or upright, every corner at least 15° from pointing straight along an axis.
Rim
5.5 on the right, 4.4 across the back, 5.4 on the left, measured at the middle of each side. The back is thinnest because it is furthest away.
Back wall
4.3 between the rim and the melt at the back, 2.5 on the left, 0.5 on the right, and none at the near corner, where the near wall hides it.
Lip
The rim's outer wall shows 2.4 deep below the near left edge. The right wall shows under one unit from this eye, so it is left out rather than drawn as a fringe.
One colour
The back wall opens to the ground and a constant gap cuts the rim away from the lip and the melt: 2 units on the master, 1 px on the cuts.
Colours
Dark: rim #eef1f5, lip #959ca8, back wall #5a616d. Light: rim #0b0d12, lip and back wall #7d848f. Melt #ff6b1a in both.

Three drawings

The small cuts are drawn for their size, the way a type family has optical sizes. They keep the master's outline and turn, with the three corners moved onto whole pixels, and then set every stroke in pixels rather than scaling the master down.

32 px: corners at (23, 27), (30, 2), (2, 8); rim 3 px on the sides and 2.5 across the back, back wall 2 px, lip 3 px. 16 px: corners at (12, 13), (15, 1), (1, 4); rim 1.75 px on the sides and 1.5 across the back, back wall 1 px, lip 1.5 px. A 2 px rim at 16 px left a melt too small to see, so the rim is thinner in proportion there. Moving the corners onto pixels changes the turn a little: edges are at least 12° and 12° from an axis at 32 and 16, against 12° on the master.

Master at 224 px; the 32 and 16 cuts rendered at their own size and enlarged 8× and 16×, one square per pixel.

Size ladder

Each size uses the drawing made for it: the 16 cut below 24 px, the 32 cut up to 47 px, the master from 48 px.

One colour

The idea survives in one colour: a solid rim, the melt as a solid inside it, and the back wall left open. On the master and the 32 cut the lip stays, cut away from the rim by the same gap; the 16 cut drops it, since a 1 px gap and a 1 px lip merge into grey.

mark-mono.svg at 128 px, mark-mono-32.svg and mark-mono-16.svg at their own size, in currentColor.

Lockup

The wordmark is lowercase, so the mark is set to the ascender: the rim's highest corner touches the ascender line, and the lip's lowest point overshoots the baseline by 30 units, as pointed letters do.

The gap to the word is 180 units, measured from the rim's right corner, the widest point; the right side falls away from the c below it, so the gap is set tight. Keep clear space of one x-height on every side. Below 40 px tall, set the 16 cut beside live 15 px text instead, as in the top bar further down.

lockup-dark.svg, lockup-light.svg and lockup-mono.svg (currentColor) at 48 px tall.

In context

Browser tabs show favicon.svg, which draws the 16 cut below 24 px and switches its colours with the system theme; favicon.ico carries the light 16 and 32 cuts for the rest. The dashboard bar is 52 px with the 16 cut beside 15 px text. The README header uses the lockup files, swapped by a picture element.

First glance

Each image, alone at its real size, was shown to a fresh model session with no context and the question “what does this icon depict?”. Its first answer is quoted. Turned 15°, no first read is a downvote arrow or a yield sign, but first impressions still reach for an interface glyph: a warning marker, a play button, a caret, a cursor. The specific guesses named a crucible or funnel, though the session ran inside this project and may have been primed by its name.
